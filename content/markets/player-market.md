---
title: Player markets
desc: Auction houses, player shops and trading. How to make them lively, fair and drain a little money.
---

A player market is where the real economy happens. When it works, prices come from what players want. You mostly watch and protect it.

## The tools

| Tool | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Auction house** | Players list items for sale, others buy or bid | Easy trading, searching, no need to be online |
| **Player shops** (chest shops or shop GUIs) | Players set up shops in the world | Community feel, shopping districts |
| **Direct trade** | Players trade face to face | Rare deals, but easy to scam |
| **Bounty or request board** | Players post what they want to buy | Niche items |

Offer at least one of the first two. The auction house is easiest to track and tax.

## Keep the market healthy

### Listing fees and sale fees

A small fee when listing and a larger one when sold keeps the market clean.
- **Listing fee** (1% to 3%) stops spam and test listings.
- **Sale fee** (3% to 8%) is your main marketplace sink.

### Limit listings

A cap per player (for example 10 to 20 active listings) prevents one person flooding the market and keeps the database light.

### Price floors on admin-backed items

If a player lists wheat for 1 coin and the admin shop buys it for 2, players will buy the cheap wheat and flip it. Either block listings below the admin sell price or accept that the admin price is the floor.

### Expiry

Listings should expire after a few days. Dead listings clutter the market and hide real prices.

### Search and sort

A market that is hard to browse is a market that does not work. Good search and "sort by price" matter more than you think.

## Player shops and land

If players can create shops, make shop space an asset:
- Shop plots cost money or rent.
- Rent grows with location (spawn is more valuable).
- Limit the number of shops per player.

That turns a place to trade into a sink and a reason to compete for prime spots.

## Preventing monopolies

A monopoly happens when one player controls most of an item. Watch for:

- One player buying out every item of a type and relisting higher
- Cartels agreeing not to undercut each other

Possible tools:
- Per-player buy limits on rare items
- Admin shop as a ceiling for common items
- Higher fees for very large purchases
- Clear rules against collusion, enforced fairly

## Scams and safety

- Make trades confirm clearly with both sides seeing the same item.
- Use a GUI, not chat commands, for trades.
- Log trades. A log makes disputes quick.
- Have a clear refund policy: usually none for honest mistakes, yes for bugs.

> [!TIP] Seed the market
> A new market looks empty. Staff or a few friendly players can list useful items at fair prices so new players learn how it works. Keep that stock small, honest and logged.

## What to watch in the numbers

| Metric | Healthy | Worrying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of listings | Steady or growing | Falling to nothing |
| Items sold / items listed | 50% or more | Under 20% (prices too high) |
| Price vs admin buy price | Above the admin sell price, below the admin buy price | Outside that range |
| Fees collected | Stable share of trade volume | Falling (trading outside the market) |
