---
title: Progression and the wealth curve
desc: How fast should players get rich? Shape the early, middle and late game so there is always a next goal.
---

Money is only interesting when there is something to aim for. Your job is to make sure a player always has a goal that is **just out of reach, but visible**.

## The three stages

| Stage | Hours played | Player goals | What the economy should do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 0 to 20 | Tools, first base, first rank | Quick wins. Prices low, earnings steady |
| Middle | 20 to 150 | Upgrades, bigger builds, better gear | Choices matter. Real costs, real decisions |
| Late | 150+ | Prestige, collections, status | Big sinks. Cosmetics, titles, large plots, limited items |

## The wealth curve

A healthy economy has income that grows with time **but more slowly than prices of the things players want**. Plot your price ladder:

- First rank costs about 1 to 2 hours of early income
- Second rank costs about 3 to 5 hours
- Later ranks cost 10, 25, 60 hours and up

If the ladder is too flat, players finish it in a weekend. If it is too steep, they give up. Ask a few real players how long each step felt.

## Catch-up and slow down

Two mechanics keep the gap between new and old players healthy:

**Catch-up (for new players)**
- Bonus rewards from early quests
- Small multipliers for the first X hours
- Cheap starter items that do not scale with wealth

**Slow-down (for rich players)**
- Costs that rise with wealth, like a rank price or a plot upkeep
- Sinks aimed at big balances, like a wealth tax with an exemption
- Items that cannot be mass bought (limited stock, limited per player)

> [!RULE] Rule of thumb
> The richest 10% of players should hold under 50% of all money. Over 70% means new players are locked out and the market belongs to a few.

## The endgame problem

Late-game players have everything. Without sinks, their money piles up and bids prices up in the player market. Give them things to spend on that are **unique or limited**:

- Cosmetics and titles
- Seasonal items that return in smaller numbers
- Large plots, island upgrades and special builds
- Server-wide projects they can fund together
- Prestige systems that reset a little progress for a permanent bonus

## Seasons and resets

If your server runs in seasons, a reset is the ultimate sink: it brings every balance back to start. It is powerful, but it costs player trust. Rules for fair resets:

1. Announce the date weeks ahead.
2. Keep cosmetics, titles or a "hall of fame" so effort is remembered.
3. Fix the economy problems that caused the reset, or they return in a month.

More in [Fixing inflation](inflation/fixing.md).

## Planning notes

- A written price ladder for ranks, upgrades and plots
- A catch-up mechanic for new players
- At least three late-game sinks that are worth buying
- A plan for what the top player does with their money
