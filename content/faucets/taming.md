title: Taming faucets
desc: Practical ways to cap, slow and shape income without making the game feel stingy.
---

You rarely need to remove a faucet. You usually need to **shape** it, so the first hour pays well and the thousandth hour does not explode.

## The six levers

### 1. Caps

A cap says "this much per hour or per day". It is simple and it works.

- **Per action:** a job pays up to X per minute.
- **Per day:** a player can sell up to Y coins of an item at full price.
- **Per week:** limited rewards such as boss payouts.

> [!TIP] Make caps visible
> Players accept caps they can see. Show a message like "Daily sell limit reached, resets at midnight". A hidden cap feels like a bug.

### 2. Diminishing returns

Instead of a wall, use a slope. Each sale in a day pays a bit less than the one before. Players still feel progress, and a farm that runs for 20 hours earns much less than 20 times a single hour. Formulas are in [Formulas](toolkit/formulas.md).

### 3. Cooldowns

A cooldown spreads rewards over time. Daily rewards, bounties and bosses all benefit. Keep cooldowns long enough that checking in does not become a chore.

### 4. Requirements

Gate large rewards behind playtime, rank or progress. A reward for "first 5 hours online" stops brand-new alt accounts from collecting it.

### 5. Scaling with progress

A quest that pays 500 coins is generous on day one and silly at hour 500. Scale rewards to a player's stage, or switch them to non-coin rewards later in the game.

### 6. Pay in items, not coins

An item is only worth something once it has been used or sold. That gives you a second chance to control it, through shop limits and sinks. A reward of "a stack of food and a repair token" is far safer than 5,000 coins.

## Anti-AFK and automation

AFK and auto-clicker income is the most common reason faucets run away.

- Use an AFK detector. Stop paying rewards while a player is idle.
- Pay less for repeated identical actions.
- Limit rewards for killing mobs from a grinder (many plugins have a "spawner mob" setting).
- Block or limit auto-clickers with an anti-cheat.
- Treat unusual income as a signal. Check any account earning more than 3 to 5 times the server's average.

> [!WARNING] Alt accounts
> Anyone can create extra accounts for daily rewards, votes and starting balances. Link rewards to playtime, use per-IP limits where fair and review the richest new accounts weekly.

## A simple budget method

1. Decide what an average active player should earn in an hour (the hourly rate).
2. Decide the **mix**: for example 50% from selling, 25% from jobs, 15% from quests and 10% from other sources.
3. Set caps so that a player who maxes every faucet earns at most about **1.5x** the hourly rate.
4. Check the real numbers each week. If one faucet is above its share, tighten it.

## Quick fixes when a faucet is too strong

| Symptom | First fix |
| --- | --- |
| One item sells for far too much | Lower the sell price, add a daily cap |
| One job dominates | Cut its pay, add diminishing returns |
| Overnight income spikes | Anti-AFK, check for farms and auto-clickers |
| A new event doubled money | Cap the event reward, or end it early with a note |
| New accounts earn a lot | Raise playtime requirements for rewards |
