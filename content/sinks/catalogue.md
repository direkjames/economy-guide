A sink **destroys coins**. The best sinks are the ones players choose gladly because they get something they want. The worst are hidden fees that feel like theft.

## The best sinks, ranked

| Sink | Fun factor | Drain power | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetics, titles, pets | High | Medium | Prestige spending. Great for late game |
| Land: plots, claims, island upgrades | High | High | Scales with progress |
| Rank-ups and unlocks | High | High | Clear goals, one-off |
| Repairs, enchant and anvil costs | Medium | Medium | Constant, everyone uses it |
| Shop buy prices and spreads | Medium | High | The quiet workhorse |
| Rent and upkeep | Medium | High | Recurring sink that matches ownership |
| Auction and trade fees | Low | Medium | Scales with market activity |
| Teleport, command and kit costs | Medium | Low to medium | Convenience sink |
| Casino and games | Medium | High | Must be fair and capped. Lose limits help |
| Limited-stock items | High | Medium | Creates real demand |
| Seasonal or event shops | High | Medium | Time-limited, easy to sell |
| Wealth or idle tax | Low | High | Targets hoarding, keep rules simple |
| Death cost | Low | Low | Can feel harsh. Use carefully |

## Good sinks versus bad sinks

A **good sink** has a reason, a reward and clear rules:
- Players see the price before paying.
- They get something they value.
- Rich players pay more than new players.

A **bad sink** punishes players for playing:
- Hidden fees.
- Tax on every small action.
- Costs that hit new players harder than rich ones.
- "Pay or lose your stuff" rules.

> [!RULE] The fun test
> Would a player tell a friend about this purchase? Cosmetics, plots and ranks pass. A 10% tax on moving items does not.

## Sink ideas by type

### Prestige sinks (rich players)
- Cosmetic particle trails, hats, tags, titles
- Pets and mounts
- Large builds or exclusive plots
- Name or color rights, banners and statues
- Leaderboard fame (donating to a community project)

### Progress sinks (everyone)
- Rank-up costs
- Skill or job levels that cost coins to unlock
- Island or plot expansions
- Storage and home slot upgrades
- Kit upgrades

### Convenience sinks (everyone)
- Warp and teleport fees
- Repair and rename costs
- Shop fees
- Mail or storage fees (small)

### Recurring sinks
- Plot rent
- Shop rent for player shops
- Upkeep for claims or islands

Recurring sinks are powerful because they match ownership. Someone with a huge base pays more over time than someone with a cabin.

### Time-limited sinks
- Seasonal shops with items that rotate
- Events with entry fees and prizes (careful: prizes are faucets)
- Auctions of rare items run by the server, with the winning bid destroyed

### Gambling
A well-run casino destroys coins because of the **house edge**. Keep it small, cap bets and set daily loss limits. Be careful: it can hurt vulnerable players and attract exploits. See [Stopping exploits](fair/exploits.md).

## How much sink do you need?

If your faucets create 100,000 coins per day, your sinks should destroy 80,000 to 100,000. If you are not sure, measure:

1. Add up what each sink removed last week, using your logs.
2. Compare it to what faucets created.
3. If you fall short, raise a sink or add a new one. See [Fixing inflation](inflation/fixing.md).
