---
title: Fairness
desc: How to welcome new players, avoid pay-to-win, share information openly and change the rules without anger.
---

A fair economy is one where **effort is rewarded, rules are clear and nobody wins by cheating or by money alone**. Players forgive almost everything except feeling cheated.

## The fairness checklist

- New players can reach the first goals within their first session.
- Veterans cannot buy their way past everything on day one of a season.
- Rules for money are public: rates, caps, fees.
- Staff follow the same rules as players.
- Exploits are handled the same way for everyone.
- Changes are announced before they happen.

## Welcoming new players

Money is the least of a newcomer's worries, but a bad first week ends many servers.

- **Starter kit** with tools and food, small enough that it does not feed alts.
- **A short quest chain** that teaches the economy: sell something, buy something, find the market.
- **Cheap first goals**: the first rank or first plot should take an hour or two.
- **A protected price range**: items a newcomer needs (food, torches, basic tools) stay cheap through the admin shop.
- **Friendly rewards for helping**: a veteran who guides a newbie earns a small cosmetic, not coins.

> [!RULE] Help, do not feed
> A big gift of money makes the first week easy and the second week confusing. Give things to do and things to buy, not piles of coins.

## Avoiding pay-to-win

Players tolerate cosmetics and convenience for sale. They hate buying power.

| Fine | Not fine |
| --- | --- |
| Cosmetic ranks, tags, particles | Selling in-game coins or items for real money |
| Convenience (extra homes, more storage) with modest limits | Real-money stat boosts or exclusive powerful gear |
| Supporter perks that save time, not money | Faster money income for payers |

If you do sell perks, keep a clear line between "looks" and "power", and check Mojang's current rules.

## Gaps between rich and poor

Some difference is natural and fun. Too much kills the game.

- Track the **top 10% share** and keep it below 50% to 60%.
- Use sinks that scale with wealth: rent, upkeep, luxury goods.
- Create ways for newer players to earn well from niche jobs (farming, rare drops, services), not only from long grinding.
- Put some of the best rewards behind **skill or effort**, not money.

## Be open about the rules

- Publish tax rates, daily limits and rank prices on your wiki or rules page.
- Explain **why** a rule exists, in one sentence.
- Share a short economy report each month. Players love seeing the numbers.

## Changing things fairly

1. Give notice: at least a few days for small changes, a week or more for big ones.
2. Explain the reason in plain words.
3. Protect past effort where you can: grandfather items players already bought, or refund a price drop.
4. Do not retroactively punish players for using a system you built.

### Template: a rule change post

> **What is changing:** Sell prices for X drop by 30%.
> **When:** Monday, 18:00 server time.
> **Why:** Farms were creating too much money and prices were rising.
> **How we are making up for it:** New daily quests, and a limited-time shop.
> **Questions:** Ask in the staff channel or reply here.

## Staff and money

- Staff do not give themselves or friends money or items. If you need to test something, use a clearly marked test account and log it.
- Admin money commands are limited to a few trusted people and always logged.
- Staff may not profit from knowing about a price change before it is announced.
- Giveaways follow a written budget.

## Handling complaints

When a player says "it is not fair", ask three questions:

1. **Is it a bug or a rule?** If a bug, fix and compensate fairly.
2. **Who is affected?** Everyone, or a few? A rule that hurts many is a design issue.
3. **Can we explain it simply?** If you cannot, the rule may be too complex.
